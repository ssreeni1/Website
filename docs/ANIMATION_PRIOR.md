# Animation Prior

This is the design and engineering prior for every technical animation on the
site. It is intentionally more abstract than the Formula and backgammon scenes.
The next scene should inherit this system without inheriting either scene's
subject matter.

The standard is not “a cool object moving.” The standard is:

> A real system, made legible through motion, whose geometry, data, camera, and
> instrumentation all describe the same state.

If an effect cannot be explained by the state of the system, it is probably
decoration and should be removed.

## 1. The experience model

Every scene has five layers:

1. **Subject** — the thing whose state is changing.
2. **World** — the constraint or environment that gives the motion meaning.
3. **Mechanics** — the visible parts that respond to the subject's state.
4. **Instrumentation** — the numbers and labels that explain what is happening.
5. **Analysis** — a temporary reveal that shows structure normally hidden.

The scene works only when all five layers agree.

Examples:

- A vehicle does not merely translate. Its heading follows the track, its wheels
  steer and spin, its chassis reacts, and its readouts describe that same instant.
- A checker does not merely travel between coordinates. It leaves the top of a
  real stack, follows a legal move, changes the board state, and updates every
  derived metric.

## 2. Start with a thesis, not an asset

Before choosing a model or drawing a board, write one sentence:

> “This visual makes **[system]** understandable by showing **[relationship]**
> changing through **[motion]**.”

The thesis must name a relationship, not just an object.

Weak:

> A satellite rotating in space.

Strong:

> A satellite maintaining attitude while reaction-wheel momentum, sun angle,
> and power generation compete over one orbit.

The thesis determines the motion, data, camera, and telemetry. If those elements
do not reinforce the thesis, the scene will feel like a model viewer.

## 3. One state, many projections

Maintain one authoritative state for the scene. Geometry, telemetry, captions,
highlights, probabilities, and timelines are projections of that state.

```text
source data or deterministic rules
              ↓
      authoritative state
       ↙      ↓       ↘
 geometry   telemetry   analysis
       ↘      ↓       ↙
         one rendered frame
```

Do not independently animate the HUD and the subject until they merely look
synchronized. Derive both from the same time and state.

Use three explicit data classes:

- **Recorded** — values taken directly from a named source.
- **Derived** — values calculated from recorded or exact state.
- **Simulated** — values generated by a declared model.

Label them honestly in the interface. A plausible number is not an accurate
number.

## 4. Motion hierarchy

Build motion in this order.

### 4.1 World motion

Establish where the subject is and what constrains it: track, board, orbit,
network, field, timeline, or another spatial system.

### 4.2 Subject motion

Move the primary rig through the world. Position and orientation should come
from the same path or state transition.

### 4.3 Mechanical response

Articulate parts around their real pivots:

- wheels steer around steering axes and spin around axles;
- hinges rotate around hinges;
- stacks remove their most recently placed visible element;
- linkages inherit the transforms of the parts they connect.

Mechanical response is what turns translation into believable motion.

### 4.4 Event motion

Use a finite motion phrase for discrete events:

1. anticipation;
2. lift or separation;
3. travel;
4. settlement;
5. recalculation.

Events need a readable beginning and end. Continuous systems should not look
like a sequence of events, and discrete events should not drift endlessly.

### 4.5 Analytical reveal

Scans, internal layers, trajectories, and probability spaces should appear as a
temporary explanatory mode. They must reveal a relationship that the normal
view cannot show.

An analytical reveal is not a screensaver. It should have a subject, a sweep or
focus, and a reason to end.

## 5. Continuous motion rules

Render at the browser's native animation cadence with
`requestAnimationFrame`. Do not make motion advance by “one frame”; advance it
by elapsed time.

Use delta-time damping:

```ts
const alpha = 1 - Math.exp(-response * delta);
value += (target - value) * alpha;
```

This is stable across different refresh rates. A fixed per-frame factor is not.

For sparse or irregular data:

1. clean obvious discontinuities;
2. smooth measurement noise without changing the underlying path;
3. resample a dense closed or open curve;
4. render both the world geometry and the moving subject from that same curve.

Never render a track from raw line segments while moving the subject on a
different spline. The mismatch will be visible as cornering jitter.

Use continuous angular interpolation:

```ts
const difference = Math.atan2(
  Math.sin(targetAngle - currentAngle),
  Math.cos(targetAngle - currentAngle),
);
currentAngle += difference * alpha;
```

For discrete travel, use an eased curve such as `smootherstep` over a Bezier or
another path with a deliberate arc. Do not linearly teleport between stack
coordinates.

At a loop boundary, verify position, tangent, state, and telemetry continuity.
The final frame and first frame should be valid neighbors.

## 6. Coordinate and hierarchy rules

Transform hierarchy is a design decision.

```text
scene
├── world
│   ├── ground or constraint
│   └── history / prediction
└── subject rig
    ├── body
    ├── articulated part
    │   └── part-local highlight
    ├── articulated part
    └── internal analysis
```

Rules:

- A highlight for a moving part is a child of that part.
- A brake disc belongs to the wheel or upright, not the scene.
- A scan volume belongs to the subject rig.
- A trajectory belongs to the world.
- A pivot comes from the real joint or authored geometry.
- Never compensate for a hierarchy error with a hand-tuned screen offset.
- Derive placement from geometry bounds, board coordinates, or state topology
  whenever possible.

If an overlay appears detached from its target in any camera angle, its parent,
pivot, or coordinate space is wrong.

## 7. Camera prior

The default camera must explain the thesis before the user interacts.

Choose it by asking:

- What relationship should be understood first?
- Which face or axis establishes the subject?
- Does the complete subject fit with intentional negative space?
- Are the most important mechanical parts visible?
- Does the world read as a real constraint rather than a background texture?

The camera should:

- start from a deliberate yaw, pitch, and distance;
- use time-based damping;
- orbit around a stable semantic target;
- clamp pitch and zoom to useful ranges;
- preserve a deterministic reset;
- remain usable while the simulation continues.

Test the default, two oblique views, the highest and lowest useful pitch, both
zoom limits, and reset.

## 8. Visual language

The site uses a restrained technical grammar:

- paper is the field;
- ink carries structure;
- red carries live state, causality, or selection;
- muted gray carries context.

Red is scarce. Every red element must answer one of these questions:

- What is active?
- What just changed?
- What is being measured?
- Where will the system go next?

Remove red elements that merely make the frame feel more “technical.”

Keep telemetry outside the motion viewport. Panels may align with the viewport
edges, but they should never cover the subject. Within the viewport, allow only
interaction guidance and scene-native analysis.

Avoid:

- floating bars with no physical anchor;
- decorative vectors that read as geometry;
- repeated outlines competing with the subject;
- labels crossing moving parts;
- panels whose alignment changes between scenes;
- transparent layers that destroy depth ordering.

## 9. Numeric and logical truth

Accuracy is part of the visual finish.

Before animating, define the invariants:

- total object count;
- legal transitions;
- conservation rules;
- bounds and units;
- source sampling rate;
- exact probabilities;
- recorded versus derived values.

Recalculate derived values from state after every transition. Do not maintain
separate counters that can drift from the rendered scene.

For stacks and queues, store ordered collections. A visual stack cannot be
reconstructed safely from global object order.

For random processes, decide whether the sequence is:

- truly random;
- seeded and reproducible;
- or an authored deterministic demonstration.

The interface should not imply one while implementing another.

## 10. Telemetry prior

Telemetry is an explanation layer, not dashboard decoration.

Each scene should have:

- one small primary state block;
- one structural or spatial readout;
- one derived interpretation;
- provenance or model labeling when relevant.

Telemetry should change at the cadence of the phenomenon it describes. Rapid
channels may update frequently; strategic metrics should update only when the
state changes.

The center visual must remain legible without reading the telemetry. The
telemetry should make the visual more precise, not rescue an unclear visual.

## 11. Interaction prior

Orbit, zoom, and reset are part of the animation, not debug controls.

Interactions must:

- work while the simulation is moving;
- preserve the subject's transform hierarchy;
- use pointer capture so drags do not break at the frame edge;
- support keyboard equivalents;
- stop automatic scene cycling once the user takes control;
- never expose a camera angle where overlays detach or stacks collapse.

Interaction text should be brief and consistent across scenes.

## 13. Surgical quality gate

Do not publish until every gate passes.

### Geometry

- No interpenetration that is not physically intended.
- No disconnected joints, wheels, markers, or overlays.
- Every moving part uses the correct pivot.
- Stacks, tracks, and repeated geometry stay aligned from every camera.

### Motion

- No frame-stepped translation or heading changes.
- No abrupt change at data-sample boundaries.
- No reversal or snap during settlement.
- Articulated parts agree with the subject's motion and telemetry.
- The loop boundary is continuous.

### Composition

- The default camera communicates the thesis.
- The complete subject fits inside the frame.
- Red is reserved for meaningful state.
- Telemetry panels align with the motion frame and never overlap it.
- Analytical reveals remain readable without flattening the scene.

### Truth

- Counts, probabilities, units, and state transitions are correct.
- Captions describe the rendered frame, not a nearby frame.
- Recorded, derived, and simulated values are labeled.

### Interaction

- Drag, zoom, keyboard orbit, and reset work in both normal and analysis modes.
- Extreme allowed camera angles expose no detached geometry.
- Scene switching resets cleanly.

### Production

- Build and automated tests pass.
- The deployed asset hashes match the new release.
- A cache-bypassed production reload is visually checked.
- Default, oblique, analysis, and high-motion frames are inspected live.

## 14. Ship blockers

The following are always blockers, even when the prototype is otherwise strong:

- a moving element that is not driven by authoritative state;
- a number that is decorative or knowingly inaccurate;
- an overlay that detaches in an allowed view;
- an object pulled from the middle of a visible stack;
- a path rendered differently from the path being followed;
- a camera default that crops the subject;
- a telemetry panel overlapping the motion frame;
- a transition that looks good only in a still image;
- a regression discovered after deployment that could have been caught by an
  interaction check.

The standard is coherence. Detail is useful only when every detail belongs to
the same system.
